Experimental Methods for Testing Materials Using Rotational Forces

Development of a rotating experimental platform for high-throughput material testing, combining flexure-guided loading with Hall-effect sensing and camera-based displacement measurements.

Project Overview

This project focused on the development of experimental methods for characterizing materials in a rotating environment. Instead of imposing displacement with a conventional testing machine, the rotating setup generates force through centrifugal loading, making displacement measurement essential for characterizing the mechanical response.

Two complementary measurement approaches were implemented and compared: an embedded Hall-effect sensor combined with a permanent magnet, and an optical tracking method based on an industrial camera. The objective was to evaluate their suitability for future fatigue testing and rupture detection applications.

Supervision Advisor: Mario Ibrahim · Supervisor: Dr. Pedro Reis
Main hardware ETEL direct-drive motor, Arduino MKR WiFi 1010, Raspberry Pi, IDS uEye CP camera and DRV5013 Hall sensor.
Experimental range Target angular velocities from 10 to 50 rad/s with flexure lengths from 20 mm to 50 mm.
Project goal Develop a reliable displacement measurement framework for rotating fatigue test rigs.

Experimental Setup

The experimental apparatus consists of a rotating acrylic structure carrying four testing stations mounted symmetrically around the motor axis. Each station uses a flexure-guided carriage so that the moving mass is constrained to radial translation under centrifugal loading.

  • Four identical testing stations mounted on a 205 mm pitch circle diameter
  • Flexure lengths of 20, 30, 40 and 50 mm
  • Constant flexure thickness of 0.20 mm and width of 13.00 mm
  • Specimens mounted on a dedicated carriage with break-off supports

Measurement Methods

Two non-contact acquisition methods were retained and experimentally compared for use in the rotating frame. The Hall-effect sensor provides an embedded sensing solution, while the camera-based method serves as an independent reference measurement.

  • Hall-effect sensor + permanent magnet for embedded displacement sensing
  • Industrial camera tracking of a marker attached to the carriage
  • Offline image processing for displacement reconstruction
  • Comparison of both methods across multiple motor velocity profiles

Main Results

Both measurement techniques successfully captured the displacement evolution of the moving carriage. Larger displacements were observed at higher rotational velocities and for longer flexures, consistent with the increase in centrifugal loading and the reduction in structural stiffness.

The camera-based measurements provided the most reliable quantitative reference, while the Hall-effect sensor reproduced the main displacement trends with good agreement. The main limitation of the Hall-effect approach remains the calibration procedure, which depends strongly on the sensor–magnet configuration.

Dynamic Characterization

The dynamic response of the flexure system was also characterized through free oscillation experiments. The natural frequency decreases as the flexure length increases, confirming the expected reduction in stiffness for longer compliant elements.

  • 30 mm flexure: natural frequency ≈ 33.25 Hz
  • 50 mm flexure: natural frequency ≈ 16.11 Hz
  • Stable displacement evolution without significant overshoot
  • Useful basis for selecting safe operating conditions away from resonance

Conclusion

The project demonstrated the feasibility of monitoring displacement directly on a rotating test rig using both embedded and optical measurement techniques. The results validated the general behaviour of the setup and confirmed the influence of rotational speed and flexure stiffness on the system response.

The Hall-effect sensor is promising for compact in-situ monitoring, but further calibration improvements are required before full quantitative validation in future fatigue testing applications.

Future Work

  • Improve Hall-effect calibration using synchronized displacement and voltage measurements
  • Integrate lighting directly on the rotating frame for more robust optical tracking
  • Extend the methodology to real specimens and rupture detection under centrifugal loading
  • Use the platform for future fatigue characterization campaigns
