Data-Driven Optimization of Lattice Structures

Development of a complete parametric design, finite element simulation and surrogate-assisted optimization pipeline to identify lightweight lattice structures with the best mass–strength trade-off.

Project Overview

This project investigated the data-driven optimization of lightweight lattice structures under compression. The objective was to automatically explore a mixed design space combining geometric and topological variables while limiting the number of expensive three-dimensional finite element simulations.

A fully automated Python workflow was developed to generate lattice geometries, mesh them, perform finite element simulations and extract structural performance data. Gaussian Process surrogate models were then combined with NSGA-II multi-objective optimization to recover the Pareto-optimal trade-off between mass and mechanical resistance.

Structure 50 mm cubic PLA lattice subjected to uniaxial compression.
Design variables Strut radius, number of cells per axis and unit-cell topology.
Numerical tools Python, Gmsh, DOLFINx, scikit-learn, pymoo and Fusion for model verification.
Optimization objective Minimize lattice mass and von Mises stress simultaneously.

Automated Simulation Pipeline

Each candidate lattice was evaluated through a fully automated three-stage workflow. This allowed hundreds of design configurations to be generated and analysed consistently without manual CAD or FEM setup.

  • Parametric geometry generation in Gmsh using OpenCASCADE primitives
  • Automatic tetrahedral mesh generation and export
  • Linear-elastic FEM simulation in DOLFINx
  • Automatic extraction of displacement, von Mises stress and safety factor

FEM Model Verification

Before creating the optimization dataset, the Python DOLFINx model was verified against a reference simulation performed in Fusion. Boundary conditions, applied load and resulting structural response were compared to ensure consistency.

The comparison showed an error below 7%, which was considered sufficient to validate the simulation pipeline for the subsequent data-driven optimization study.

Design of Experiments

The final design space contained three variables: strut radius, cell subdivision and topology. Seven unit-cell topologies were retained after removing configurations that added mass without improving axial load transfer or caused recurring mesh failures.

  • Radius: 1.0–2.0 mm, constrained by geometric feasibility
  • Cells per axis: n = 1, 2 or 3
  • Seven retained topology families
  • 16 Latin Hypercube samples per topology, for 112 FEM evaluations

Surrogate-Based Optimization

Gaussian Process models were trained independently for mass and stress for each topology. The resulting surrogate models were then used as inexpensive substitutes for the FEM solver during the optimization stage.

  • Gaussian Process regression for mass and stress
  • Log-transformed stress response to reduce the influence of extreme peaks
  • NSGA-II multi-objective optimization
  • 8,000 surrogate evaluations instead of thousands of additional FEM runs

Main Results

The optimized Pareto front showed that the simplest orthogonal lattice topology consistently dominated the useful mass range. More complex diagonal and body-reinforced configurations generally increased mass without producing enough reduction in stress under uniaxial compression.

Representative Pareto candidates were re-evaluated with the full finite element pipeline, confirming that the surrogate optimization correctly identified the dominant design family. This behaviour remained consistent across load cases from 25 to 75 kg and across different stress criteria.

112 High-fidelity FEM evaluations used to build the dataset.
8,000 Surrogate evaluations performed during NSGA-II optimization.
< 7% Error between the Python FEM model and the Fusion reference simulation.
25–75 kg Load range over which the dominant design family remained robust.

Pareto Optimization

The Pareto fronts clearly highlight the trade-off between structural mass and peak stress. Low-mass designs are more material-efficient but experience higher stress, whereas heavier structures reduce stress at the cost of increased material usage.

Across the investigated design space, the non-diagonal orthogonal topology remained the most efficient solution under axial compression.

Selected Optimal Design

The final design was selected from the verified Pareto set using a weighted criterion favouring mass reduction while maintaining structural safety.

  • Strut radius: 1.264 mm
  • Cell subdivision: n = 2
  • Topology: orthogonal, without diagonal reinforcement
  • Mass: 7.98 g
  • Von Mises stress: 7.84 MPa
  • Safety factor: 3.82
